# NextSlot Marketing Website: Phases and Visitor Journey

## Purpose

Make the NextSlot marketing website feel simple to understand and easy to act on. A visitor should be able to answer five questions in order:

1. **Is this for a business like mine?**
2. **How does it work?**
3. **Has it helped a real business?**
4. **What does it cost?**
5. **How do I get started?**

The experience should feel like one calm, coherent explanation, not five separate sales pitches. Preserve NextSlot's existing voice, visual identity, core copy, warm editorial palette, and real product evidence. Simplify the path, not the product story.

## The simplest effective journey

### 1. Help the visitor recognise themselves

**Visitor question:** “Is this for me?”

State clearly that NextSlot is for independent service businesses that take bookings. Make the examples immediately relatable: beauty and wellness, barbers, photographers, tattoo artists, mobile and fixed-location services.

- Keep the existing hero message and visual identity.
- Make the audience and the core problem easy to recognise without a long list of features.
- Use one primary next step: **See how it works**.
- Avoid pushing a signup before the visitor understands the value.

**They should leave this step thinking:** “This was made for a business like mine.”

### 2. Show the working model, not a feature catalogue

**Visitor question:** “How does it work?”

Explain the product in a short, visual sequence:

1. **Record** bookings, payments and client activity in one place.
2. **Understand** what is happening in the business.
3. **Grow** with clearer opportunities and practical next steps.

Use the existing dashboard, heatmap and Nexty orb to make the explanation tangible. Keep supporting text concise and let the interface do some of the explaining. Do not turn this into a wall of feature cards.

**They should leave this step thinking:** “I can see how this fits into my working day.”

### 3. Prove it with a real business

**Visitor question:** “Has this worked for someone like me?”

Bring the PhenomeBeauty case study into the journey directly after the explanation. Keep Shu-meez's story human and specific, and distinguish verified business results from what NextSlot helped facilitate. Explain the context behind any figures so they are meaningful, not just impressive.

- Show one clear takeaway before asking visitors to read the full case study.
- Use only verified figures and clearly describe their period and meaning.
- Link to the full case study for people who want the detail.
- Never imply that one business's results are guaranteed for another.

**They should leave this step thinking:** “A real service business has used this, and I understand what changed.”

### 4. Make the price easy to evaluate

**Visitor question:** “What will this cost me, and which option fits?”

Show pricing after the visitor understands the product and has seen real-world context. Keep tier comparison scannable.

- Explain the difference between Starter, Flow and Professional in terms of the business need each tier addresses.
- Make the price, trial period and important conditions easy to compare.
- Avoid making visitors decode long feature lists or guess which plan is appropriate.
- Keep Studio clearly marked as coming soon while that remains accurate.
- Link each plan to its existing, correct signup path.

**They should leave this step thinking:** “I understand what I get, what I pay, and where I fit.”

### 5. Make starting feel like the natural next step

**Visitor question:** “What do I do now?”

End with a clear, reassuring action. Keep **Start for free** and **Talk to us** visually consistent with the existing design system.

- Explain the next step honestly, including any trial or setup expectations.
- Keep the signup path direct and avoid adding unnecessary decisions before onboarding.
- Offer Talk to us as the alternative for visitors who need reassurance.
- Keep the primary and secondary actions distinct, not competing for equal attention in every section.

**They should leave this step thinking:** “I know what happens when I click.”

## How the whole website should guide the journey

The homepage should be the simplest complete version of this story. Visitors should be able to scroll from recognition to action without having to decide which page to visit next.

Use the existing pages as supporting depth, not as competing destinations:

- **Homepage:** the complete, concise journey.
- **How it works (/demo):** a deeper product explanation for visitors who need it.
- **Case Study (/case-study/phenomebeauty):** fuller evidence and the customer's story.
- **Pricing (/pricing):** clear plan comparison and signup routes.
- **About (/about):** the people, purpose and context behind NextSlot.
- **Resources (/resources):** useful guidance for independent service businesses.
- **Login (/login):** for existing customers.
- **Start for free (/onboarding):** the direct route into signup.

Keep the current navigation. Do not add a new “journey” menu or make visitors learn a new navigation system. On the homepage, use concise section headings and restrained links to let people go deeper when they want to.

## Minimal interaction principles

1. **One dominant next action at a time.** The page can offer a secondary route, but it should be visually quieter.
2. **Answer before asking.** Explain the value before asking visitors to sign up.
3. **Progressive disclosure.** Keep the main story short; let case study, pricing and how-it-works pages provide detail.
4. **Consistent patterns.** Shared buttons, links, headings, cards and section spacing should behave and look the same across marketing pages.
5. **No artificial urgency.** Use clear information and credible proof rather than pressure, countdowns or unsupported claims.
6. **Design for scanning.** Short headings, concise copy, meaningful visuals and generous whitespace should make the story understandable at a glance.
7. **Mobile is not a reduced desktop.** Preserve legibility, sensible reading order, comfortable touch targets and clear actions at every breakpoint.
8. **Accessibility is part of simplicity.** Use readable contrast, semantic headings, keyboard-visible focus, descriptive links and reduced-motion support where relevant.

## Implementation phases

### Phase 0: Baseline audit and guardrails

**Goal:** Know what exists before changing it.

- Audit every marketing page on desktop and mobile.
- Map current page sections to the five visitor questions.
- Record duplicated or inconsistent buttons, links, headings, spacing and visual treatments.
- Check current internal links, CTA destinations, metadata, heading hierarchy, accessibility and performance.
- Capture a baseline for key conversion and technical measures.

**Deliverable:** A prioritised issue list with affected files and expected impact.

**Guardrails:** Marketing and SEO pages only. Do not change booking pages, tenant routing, payments, authentication, admin functionality or business logic. Preserve existing copy and brand decisions unless a specific change is agreed.

### Phase 1: Establish the shared design system

**Goal:** Make the existing experience feel consistent before rewriting individual pages.

- Centralise reusable marketing tokens for colour, typography, spacing, breakpoints and content widths.
- Standardise shared buttons, links, eyebrow labels, cards and CTA sections.
- Keep the warm light editorial palette and established NextSlot typography.
- Remove inconsistent one-off styling where shared components or tokens can handle it.
- Check that shared components remain legible and responsive.

**Deliverable:** A reliable set of shared styles and components used across marketing pages.

### Phase 2: Clarify the visitor journey

**Goal:** Make the order of information feel obvious.

- Align the homepage with: who it is for → how it works → real-world evidence → pricing → getting started.
- Preserve the existing core message and visual story.
- Use concise transitions so each section answers the next natural question.
- Make the primary action clear and use secondary links only where they help.
- Ensure deeper pages reinforce the same story rather than restarting it.

**Deliverable:** A coherent, minimal journey from the homepage through the existing supporting pages.

### Phase 3: Strengthen explanation and evidence

**Goal:** Help visitors understand the product and trust what they see.

- Improve the legibility and responsive scale of the dashboard preview.
- Keep the heatmap and Nexty orb as distinctive visual elements.
- Ensure product visuals support the surrounding copy rather than compete with it.
- Make the PhenomeBeauty story concise on the homepage and detailed on the case-study page.
- Verify every metric, date range and claim before publishing.

**Deliverable:** A product explanation visitors can scan and evidence they can interpret confidently.

### Phase 4: Simplify pricing and signup

**Goal:** Reduce uncertainty at the point of decision.

- Make plan differences easy to compare by outcome and business need.
- Present prices, trial periods and material conditions clearly.
- Verify that every plan CTA leads to the correct existing signup flow.
- Explain what happens after clicking Start for free.
- Keep Talk to us available without allowing it to distract from the primary action.

**Deliverable:** A pricing-to-signup path with fewer unanswered questions and no broken or misleading transitions.

### Phase 5: Accessibility, responsive and technical QA

**Goal:** Make the experience work reliably for everyone.

- Test all marketing pages at mobile, tablet, laptop and wide-desktop sizes.
- Check for overflow, clipped text, tiny visuals, awkward wrapping and excessive vertical gaps.
- Verify keyboard navigation, focus states, semantic structure, contrast, alt text and reduced-motion behaviour.
- Check internal links, canonical URLs, metadata, structured data where appropriate, image loading and Core Web Vitals.
- Run lint, type checks and production build; resolve relevant warnings as well as errors.
- Confirm that no out-of-scope app behaviour or files changed.

**Deliverable:** A verified, responsive marketing experience ready for review.

### Phase 6: Measure and refine

**Goal:** Use evidence rather than opinion to decide what to improve next.

Track a small set of measures:

- Primary CTA click-through rate.
- Engagement with the how-it-works explanation.
- Case-study visits from the homepage.
- Pricing visits and plan CTA clicks.
- Signup starts and completed signups.
- Mobile versus desktop conversion and friction.
- Search visibility and organic landing-page engagement.

Use these to identify the largest drop-off. Change one meaningful thing at a time where practical, and compare results before making further changes.

**Deliverable:** A small, prioritised improvement backlog based on observed visitor behaviour.

## Definition of done

The work is ready for sign-off when:

- A first-time visitor can identify the intended audience quickly.
- The core product model is understandable without reading every feature.
- Real-world evidence is clear, contextual and accurately represented.
- Pricing and trial conditions are easy to compare.
- The next step is obvious and the CTA destination is correct.
- Shared marketing components look consistent across pages.
- Mobile and desktop layouts are both legible and usable.
- Accessibility and technical checks pass.
- Changes remain within the agreed marketing/SEO scope.

## Decision rule

Whenever a proposed design change adds complexity, ask:

**Does this help the visitor answer one of their five questions, or help them take the next step?**

If not, remove it or defer it. The goal is not to show everything NextSlot can do. It is to help the right person understand enough, trust what they see, and confidently take the next step.
