## WhatsApp usage in South Africa

### Takeaway
WhatsApp is the dominant messaging and social platform in South Africa, with third-party estimates of roughly 24 to 30 million users. No official count was found, and the sources disagree on the exact figure. Usage is not in doubt; the precise number is.

### Cited Findings
- Yazi estimates 28 to 29 million WhatsApp users in South Africa at the end of 2025 — [Yazi, WhatsApp Penetration in Africa (2026)](https://www.askyazi.com/articles/whatsapp-penetration-across-africa-statistics-by-country) (search summary only; not fetched). Confidence: Medium.
- DataReportal estimates of 29 to 30 million users are cited by the same source. Confidence: Medium (secondary citation of DataReportal; primary page not accessible).
- About 94% of South African internet users are on WhatsApp by 2025, per DataReportal data as summarised by [Yazi](https://www.askyazi.com/useful-data-sources-for-africa/whatsapp-usage-across-africa-key-statistics-insights-for-2025) (search summary only). Confidence: Medium.
- Meltwater reports WhatsApp at 93.8% of active social media users in South Africa — [Meltwater, SA Social Media Statistics 2025](https://www.meltwater.com/en/blog/2025-social-media-statistics-south-africa) (search summary only). Confidence: Medium-Low; the same figure appears to derive from DataReportal.
- A conflicting figure of about 24 million WhatsApp users (late 2025 reach, ranked behind YouTube and Facebook) appears in a third-party summary of DataReportal data, which suggests a different measurement basis. Confidence: Low as a standalone figure.
- An older Statista projection of about 11.5 million users for 2025 is far below newer estimates and is likely outdated. Flagged as old/outlier. Confidence: Low.
- DataReportal's Digital 2027 South Africa page reports 52.2 million internet users at the end of 2026 (79.5% penetration) and 34.8 million social media user identities in October 2026; its WhatsApp chart is attributed to GWI Q2 2026, but the count was not visible in the snippet — [DataReportal, Digital 2027: South Africa](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2027-south-africa) (search summary only). Confidence: Medium for the internet figures; WhatsApp count not obtained.
- Household internet access: 85.6% of South African households had some form of internet access in 2025 per Stats SA's General Household Survey 2025 (embargoed until 26 May 2026) — [Stats SA GHS 2025](https://www.statssa.gov.za/publications/P0318/P03182025.pdf) (search summary only; PDF not fetched). Confidence: Medium.
- Household internet access rose from 28% (2010) to 78.6% (2023) per Stats SA GHS, as summarised in search results. Confidence: Medium.
- The 2025 Stats SA ICT survey was not found; the stats.gov.sa result is Saudi Arabia's GASTAT survey and was excluded. Confidence: n/a.

### Inferences
- Penetration of WhatsApp among South African internet users is very high (roughly nine in ten or more), so any client-facing channel for small providers is likely to reach most clients who are online.
- Because the headline counts come from DataReportal/GWI-type panels and model-based estimates, a fixed number should be quoted with a range, not a single figure.

### Gaps
- Could not open DataReportal Digital 2026 or 2027 South Africa pages to confirm the WhatsApp count and its source (domain blocked).
- No Meta-published South Africa WhatsApp user count was found. Meta's own country-level figures were not reachable.
- GeoPoll and World Bank data on WhatsApp or smartphone use in SA were not searched in this pass.

## WhatsApp Business adoption and small-business use

### Takeaway
WhatsApp is the top business tool for township and informal small businesses (Standard Bank, 2025), and Meta reports roughly 910,000 SMEs using its apps in 2025. No count of WhatsApp Business accounts in SA exists in the sources found.

### Cited Findings
- Standard Bank's Township Informal Economy Report (fieldwork March to May 2025; Gauteng, Western Cape, KwaZulu-Natal, Limpopo, North West; businesses with turnover R100,000 to R50m) found 74% of respondents preferred WhatsApp, followed by Instagram at 47%; websites 30%, business apps 36% — [BusinessDay, WhatsApp's the top tool for township businesses (Oct 2025)](https://www.businessday.co.za/bt/business-and-economy/2025-10-19-whatsapps-the-top-tool-for-township-businesses/) (search summary only; page blocked). Confidence: Medium (credible bank-commissioned report, but sample is township/informal SMEs, not all SA small businesses; method and sample size not verified).
- Standard Bank reports cash remains the primary method of trade for township businesses (same source). Confidence: Medium.
- A Communitas academic study (2025, 12 micro and small retailers in Gauteng) found most could not integrate the WhatsApp API because they used the free app version — [SciELO, WhatsApp Business as a digital engagement tool (2025)](https://scielo.org.za/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S2415-05252025000100002) (search summary only). Confidence: Medium for the qualitative finding; sample is very small.
- Meta-commissioned study by Public First estimates 910,000 South African SMEs used Meta's apps (Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp and others) in 2025, per summaries on [ITWeb](https://www.itweb.co.za/article/meta-platforms-contribute-r165bn-to-sas-economy/DZQ58MV8RVrvzXy2) and [Africa.com](https://africa.com/meta-drives-r16-billion-economic-impact-for-south-african-businesses/) (search summaries). It does not isolate WhatsApp Business. Confidence: Low-Medium (commissioned by Meta).
- From 1 October 2026, Meta moves WhatsApp Business Platform (API) users to per-message billing. Sources disagree on scope: [Briefly](https://briefly.co.za/people/250520-meta-charge-whatsapp-business-api-users-message-1-october-2026/) says the regular WhatsApp Business app is not charged; [StyleID](https://styleid.africa/blogs/meta-just-sent-south-african-brands-a-bill-the-real-cost-is-bigger-than-62-cents/) cites R0.62 per marketing message and R0.12 per utility message. Confidence: Medium on the change; Low on the rand prices (secondary; verify against Meta's pricing page).
- The exact number of WhatsApp Business accounts in SA is not published. One report infers millions of holders from app-store rankings (WhatsApp ranked 10th most-downloaded Android app in SA, October 2026, Similarweb, per [agenticstudio.co.za](https://agenticstudio.co.za/metas-whatsapp-business-agent-what-south-african-smbs-actually-need-to-know/)). Confidence: Low.
- A "million businesses" claim in the same source appears to be a global Meta Business Agent figure, not South African. Excluded. Confidence: n/a.

### Inferences
- Among small informal or township businesses, WhatsApp is the default channel and is preferred over websites or dedicated apps; this makes it a plausible default for client messaging, not only for marketing.
- The API-versus-app distinction matters: most micro-providers use the free app, which limits integrations (booking, reminders, automations) and may not be affected by the October 2026 billing change.

### Gaps
- No survey isolating beauty, wellness, coaching or consulting providers was found. The Standard Bank sample is township and informal businesses across sectors.
- Sample sizes and questionnaire for the Standard Bank report were not verified.

## How SA service providers arrange remote consultations today

### Takeaway
No systematic survey of how SA beauty, wellness, coaching or consulting providers run remote sessions was found. Available evidence is indirect: mental-health and telehealth studies show Zoom as the most-used video platform in a COVID-era sample, and vendor material describes WhatsApp as the default booking and communication channel for salons.

### Cited Findings
- SADAG survey (COVID-era; 200 patients and 109 mental health professionals) found Zoom was the most-used platform for online sessions: 58% of patients and 81% of professionals. SADAG states the samples are not representative — [SADAG, Is it time for telecounselling](https://www.sadag.org/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=3140:is-it-time-for-telecounselling-to-take-south-africa-s-mental-healthcare-into-the-future-insights-from-a-sadag-survey-of-patients-and-mental-health-professions&catid=149&Itemid=200) (search summary only). Confidence: Medium for the platform ranking as a sample-level finding; Low for generalisation. Dated (pandemic era).
- Vendor claim: HelloGrowthCRM says SA salon clients book through WhatsApp across urban and township areas — [HelloGrowthCRM](https://hellogrowthcrm.com/crm-for-salons-south-africa). Confidence: Low (vendor marketing, no data).
- Salon booking vendors (GetMeBooked, Inkatech) say WhatsApp bookings are added manually to the calendar and that automated WhatsApp/SMS reminders and deposits reduce no-shows — [GetMeBooked](https://www.getmebooked.co.za/), [Inkatech](https://inkatech.co.za/solutions/online-booking-system-for-salons/). These show WhatsApp is the intake channel that software is built around, but no adoption figures. Confidence: Low.
- Some salons and barbershops publish WhatsApp booking links, e.g. [Scar Hair](https://scarhair.com/book-now-via-whatsapp/) (single-business example). Confidence: n/a as evidence of prevalence.

### Inferences
- The likely default pattern is: WhatsApp for enquiry, booking and reminders; phone calls as fallback; Zoom or Google Meet used mainly by coaches and therapists for scheduled remote sessions. This is inference, not measured.

### Gaps
- No measured data on the share of SA beauty, wellness, coaching or consulting providers using WhatsApp calls versus Zoom, Google Meet, or phone for remote sessions.
- No survey of providers' interest in paid online or video consultations was found.

## Post-pandemic trends, online and video consultation demand, and barriers

### Takeaway
Telehealth-adjacent demand is real but narrower than headlines suggest: uptake among practitioners is low despite growth during COVID, willingness to pay for teleconsults is limited, and the main evidence is academic or from pandemic-era samples. Load-shedding has largely ended since May 2025, but data cost and connectivity remain barriers.

### Cited Findings
- A 2025 MDPI systematic review (via PMC) reports South Africa has the highest telemedicine adoption rate in the region studied, focused on specialist teleconsults, chronic disease and mental health; it cites 74.7% internet penetration as of January 2024 — [PMC, Telemedicine Adoption and Prospects in Sub-Sahara Africa](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11989057) (search summary only; page blocked). Confidence: Medium for the review's existence and framing; Low for the adoption ranking as reported in a summary.
- A practitioner study found actual telehealth uptake remained low, although it roughly doubled during COVID-19 — [ResearchGate, The adoption of telemedicine by healthcare practitioners in South Africa](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/387958294_The_adoption_of_telemedicine_by_healthcare_practitioners_in_South_Africa) (search summary only). Confidence: Medium.
- An academic study on private telehealth demand in SA found only 36% of respondents were willing to pay the prevailing market price for a telehealth consultation — [ScienceDirect, The demand for private telehealth services in LMICs: Evidence from South Africa](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0277953624000145) (search summary only; the 36% figure is from the summary and was not verified in the full text). Confidence: Medium-Low.
- An October 2025 market report attributes to the SAMA a claim that 70% of patients prefer virtual consultations for non-emergency issues. The original SAMA source was not found. Confidence: Low; do not quote without verification.
- The same market report cites the South African Health Review that only 22% of rural clinics have internet access. Confidence: Low (secondary citation).
- Load-shedding: the last load-shedding was 15 May 2025 (about stage 2), and Eskom passed a full year without it by May 2026, per [Callrica](https://www.callrica.co.za/why-south-africa/infrastructure-and-power/) and an FTI Consulting analysis of winter 2025 — [FTI Consulting](https://www.fticonsulting.com/insights/articles/out-darkness-lasting-economic-costs-load-shedding). Confidence: Medium for the dates; the FTI piece warns the country is still far from energy security.
- Data cost during load-shedding: a household-finance blog estimates R500 to R1,500 per month extra for a family of four at R50 to R100 per GB. No primary source. Confidence: Low.
- ICASA's inquiry found load-shedding's effect on the ICT sector was moderate at stages 2 to 4, mainly through backup power costs and service interruptions — [ICASA State of the ICT Sector 2025](https://www.icasa.org.za/uploads/files/The-State-of-the-ICT-Sector-Report-of-South-Africa-2025.pdf) (cited via search summary; the report was not fetched). Confidence: Low-Medium.
- Home fixed broadband is limited: ICASA's March 2026 report puts it at 17.4% of households; another commentary cites 20.6% of households with fixed access in 2025. These conflict. Confidence: Low; check the primary source.

### Inferences
- A video consult is most likely to be taken up where clients have stable mobile data and a smartphone. Mobile-first WhatsApp calls are therefore a better fit than desktop-based video for many SA clients, but this is inference.
- Willingness to pay (36% in one study) suggests that a paid online consult needs a clear price anchor or bundling with an in-person booking. Inference from a single study.
- The lower load-shedding risk since mid-2025 reduces one barrier, but data cost and fixed-broadband gaps are still relevant to video (as opposed to voice or text) sessions.

### Gaps
- No 2024 to 2026 survey of consumer demand for online beauty, wellness, coaching or consulting sessions in SA was found.
- The MDPI/PMC review, the ResearchGate practitioner study and the ScienceDirect willingness-to-pay study were not read in full because their hosts were blocked. Their figures come from search summaries.
- The SAMA 70% claim could not be traced to a primary source.
- Data-cost estimates during load-shedding need a primary source (e.g. Stats SA or ICASA price data).
